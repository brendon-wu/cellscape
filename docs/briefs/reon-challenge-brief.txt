Beyond the Weakest Cell: Making Every Cell Count

MIT Energy & Climate Hack 2026 | REON

 THE CHALLENGE

 Build an innovative tool that demonstrates how to use cell-level intelligence and control to optimize
 battery performance, extend system lifetime (cycle life), and unlock new opportunities, while
 quantifying the potential economic and environmental value in a specific real-world application.

How to use this Brief:
Use this brief to get oriented before building your tool. It introduces the battery concepts behind the challenge,
shows why one weak cell can limit a string, and explains the simplified capacity and retirement example.
Then use the exploration prompts and readings to choose a real application and estimate its economic or
environmental value.
There's a lot to take in, but we're excited to see what some of the world's brightest students will do with this
challenge--and we hope you have fun exploring it! : )

Battery Basics in Brief

    · Battery System Structure. A cell is one electrochemical unit. Cells are combined in series and/or
         parallel into a module; modules form a pack. A string is a series-connected path through cells or
         modules. A battery energy storage system adds power conversion, controls, thermal management, and
         other equipment around battery packs. The exact hierarchy varies by design.

    · State of charge (SOC). Think about your Phone battery. How full a cell is now relative to its present
         usable charge capacity: SOC = remaining charge ÷ current full-charge capacity × 100%. A cell at 100%
         SOC may still be aged, i.e, you can always charge you phone up to 100%, but lasting time is shorter.

    · State of health (SOH). Think about when your phone battery retire / need to be replaced. Here,
         capacity-based SOH = current full-charge capacity ÷ beginning-of-life capacity × 100%. A 70 Ah cell
         that began at 100 Ah has 70% SOH, even when fully charged.

    · Other attributes. Voltage, current, temperature, resistance, power, cycle count, and history.
    · Battery management system (BMS). Electronics and software that monitor cell or pack conditions,

         estimate states, protect operating limits, and manage charging, discharge, and balancing.

Why One Cell Can Limit a String - Weakest Cell Limitation

In a fixed series string, the same current passes through every cell. If a cell reaches its allowed charge or
discharge limit first, the string must stop that operation even when other cells have room to continue. Cell

A 16 Cell Capacity Example
Assume 16 cells were each 100 Ah at beginning of life. Today, 15 cells hold 85 Ah and one holds 70 Ah. All
begin fully charged, have comparable voltage limits, and form a fixed series string without effective energy
redistribution during the modeled discharge. Ignore resistance, temperature, and voltage variation for this
illustration.

  CELL AVERAGE

 Average cell SOH = (15 × 85% + 1 × 70%) ÷ 16 = 84.06%.

  FIXED SERIES LIMIT

 Illustrative string capacity = min(85, ..., 85, 70) Ah = 70 Ah, or 70% of its original 100 Ah capacity

The 14.06 percentage point gap compares the average cell's capacity retention with the string's simplified
capacity retention. Under the equal-capacity assumptions, 70 ÷ 84.06  83.3% is an illustrative ratio of string
capacity to average cell capacity.

What Cell Level Control Changes
In REON's architecture, we have sensing and switching at
each cell. Selective bypass can let a completed or faulty cell
leave the active path while other cells continue, when
voltage, power, thermal, and safety requirements allow. This
turns the choice of which cells participate and when into a
tool-design question. The upper bound suggested by
summing cell capacities is conceptual; a real design must
maintain a valid series voltage and meet all operating
constraints.

Make the Value Measurable

Compare the same application, system cycle life, safety constraints, and cost related with and without cell-level
control. Think about the baseline, control case, and sensitivity to uncertain assumptions. Examples areas of
interest but not limited are:

  · Usable capacity or energy recovered (Ah or kWh)
  · Additional service cycles or years
  · Economic value
  · Environmental value

A Research Starting Point
A 2026 Nature Energy study quantified the effect of cell-to-cell inconsistency in real-world EV fleets. Read the paper. It
motivates the problem, yet its EV results are not performance claims for REON or for stationary storage. Teams are
encouraged to test other applications, including data-center backup and grid-scale storage, and we are also welcome to
other innovative fields that you think your idea could apply for.

What Teams Can Assume: Given Capabilities

    · Individual-cell monitoring, including voltage, temperature, SOC, SOH, capacity, resistance, open-circuit
         voltage and performance history data.

    · Switching and selective bypass can include or remove a cell from the active string, subject to system
         limits. You can choose when to activate or bypass cells.

Build a tool on top of these capabilities. You do not need to design the switch hardware or solve low-level
control algorithms.

Directions to Explore

These directions are starting points, not boundaries. Choose any real-world application where cell-level
intelligence and control could change a useful decision. Build a tool that explores the idea based on a specific
real-world application, compares it with a clear baseline, and quantifies at least one economic or environmental
outcome. Feel free to add your assumptions and work on that.

  · Capacity recovery: estimate how much a fixed string strands and what cell-level control could make usable
    in a chosen application.

  · Lifetime optimization: schedule participation to delay an application-specific capacity or service limit while
    respecting cell stress and safety constraints.

  · Economic or environmental impact: quantify the difference against a conventional baseline for one
    stakeholder, such as an asset owner, operator, or battery integrator.

  · Possible Applications: retired EV packs, grid-scale storage, data-center battery backup, and other uses that
    benefit from uneven cell health.
